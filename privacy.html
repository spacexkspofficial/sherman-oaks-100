Privacy Policy.

We collect as little as we can: the emails you choose to send us and, only if you allow it, anonymous visit statistics.

Last updated: October 12, 2026

The short version

  • There are no accounts, forms, payments or advertising on this website.
  • If you email us, we use your message only to reply and to organize the centennial.
  • Google Analytics runs only if you select “Allow analytics.” You can change your mind at any time under Privacy choices.
  • We honor Global Privacy Control and Do Not Track signals.
  • We do not sell or share your personal information, and we do not use it for advertising.

1. Who we are and what this policy covers

This website, shermanoaks100.com, is run by the Sherman Oaks 100 Centennial Committee (“the Committee,” “we,” “us”), a group of community volunteers planning the 2027 centennial of Sherman Oaks, California. This policy explains what personal information this website and our email inbox collect, how we use it and the choices you have. It does not cover other websites that we link to.

2. Information we collect

Emails you send us

The buttons on this website (for example “Email us to volunteer” or “Suggest a place”) open a new message in your own email app, addressed to info@shermanoaks100.com. Nothing is sent until you send it yourself. When you email us, we receive your email address, your name if your email shows it, and whatever you choose to write or attach. That may include your phone number, your organization, your availability, a memory or story, or photographs.

Please do not email us sensitive information, such as Social Security, driver’s license or financial account numbers, health information or passwords. We do not need it.

Information our website host collects

This website is hosted by GitHub Pages, a service of GitHub, Inc. When you visit any page, GitHub logs your IP address and stores it for security purposes, together with standard technical details your browser sends (such as the page requested, the browser type and the date and time). GitHub handles this information under the GitHub General Privacy Statement. GitHub Pages does not give us these logs.

Google Analytics, only if you allow it

If you select “Allow analytics,” we load Google Analytics 4, a service of Google LLC, to learn how many people visit and which pages are useful. If you decline, ignore the question, or your browser sends a Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track signal, Google Analytics does not load and nothing is sent to it.

When it is allowed, Google Analytics:

  • stores two first-party cookies on your device, named _ga and _ga_ followed by an ID, containing a random identifier. We set them to expire 13 months after your most recent visit;
  • collects the pages you view, how long you stay, the link or site that brought you here, your browser, device type, screen size and language, and your approximate location (city, region and country) worked out from your IP address. Google states that Google Analytics 4 does not log or store IP addresses; and
  • gives us only combined statistics. We do not try to identify individual visitors, and we never send Google your name, email address or other information that identifies you.

We have turned off Google Analytics’ advertising features, including Google signals and ad personalization, and we keep visitor-level analytics data for the shortest period Google offers (two months). Google processes this information under the Google Privacy Policy. To learn more, see How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services.

Fonts from Google

Our pages use the Fraunces and Inter typefaces from Google Fonts. To display them, your browser requests the font files from Google, which receives your IP address, the address of the page you are reading and your browser details. Google says the Google Fonts service does not set or log cookies, and that it uses this information to deliver fonts and for security. See the Google Fonts privacy FAQ.

The community map

When you scroll to the community map, or open a link to a place on it, your browser downloads street-map images, map data and label fonts from the OpenStreetMap Foundation. Those requests include your IP address and the area of the map you are viewing. The map does not ask for or use your device’s location. See the OpenStreetMap Foundation Privacy Policy.

Information stored in your browser

This website does not set its own cookies. It keeps two small settings in your browser’s local storage, which stay on your device and are not sent to us:

  • your analytics choice (“allowed” or “declined”) and the date you made it, so we do not ask again on every page. We ask again after 12 months; and
  • while the website is in its private preview, a note that you entered the preview password.

You can delete these at any time by clearing this website’s data in your browser settings.

3. How we use information

  • To reply to your messages and questions.
  • To organize volunteers, working committees, sponsorships, partnerships and events that you ask about.
  • To consider stories, photographs and map suggestions for the centennial, and to keep a record of the permission and credit for anything we publish.
  • To understand, in combined statistics, how the website is used so we can improve it (only if you allow analytics).
  • To keep the website and our inbox secure, and to meet legal obligations.

We will not publish your name, story or photographs without your permission. When you give permission, we will confirm in writing how the material will be used and credited.

4. How we share information

We do not sell your personal information, share it for cross-context behavioral advertising, or give it to anyone for their own marketing. We share it only:

  • Within the Committee, with the volunteers and working committees who need it to respond to you. Committee members include representatives of local organizations (listed on our home page).
  • With service providers that run our tools: IONOS (our domain name and email inbox), GitHub (website hosting), Google (fonts and, only if you allow it, analytics) and the OpenStreetMap Foundation (map data). Each handles information under its own privacy policy, such as the IONOS Privacy Policy.
  • With partner organizations or event hosts only when you ask us to connect you, or with your permission.
  • When required by law, such as in response to a subpoena or court order, or to protect the safety of people or the security of the website.

Public records

Some Committee members serve on or represent public bodies, such as a neighborhood council. Messages sent to, or shared with, a public body may be public records that must be disclosed on request under the California Public Records Act, unless an exemption applies.

5. Your privacy choices

Turn on JavaScript to see or change your analytics choice. Without JavaScript, Google Analytics cannot load.

  • Analytics. Use the button above, when shown, to allow or decline Google Analytics at any time. Declining deletes the Google Analytics cookies from this website. You can also install the Google Analytics opt-out browser add-on, block or delete cookies in your browser settings, or turn on Global Privacy Control.
  • Your emails. Ask us at any time to stop contacting you, to tell you what information you have sent us, or to correct or delete it. We will do so unless we need to keep it to meet a legal obligation, for example a public-records retention rule, or to keep a record of a permission you gave us.
  • Published material. You can ask us to remove a story or photograph you contributed. We will take it off this website, although we cannot recall copies already printed or shared by others.

Send requests to info@shermanoaks100.com with the subject “Privacy request.” We may ask you to confirm that the request comes from the email address in question. We will not treat you differently for making a request.

6. Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control

If your browser sends a Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control signal, we treat it as a decline: Google Analytics does not load, and any Google Analytics cookies from this website are deleted. Other than Google Analytics, and only if you allow it, we do not allow third parties to collect information about your online activities over time and across different websites through this website.

7. How long we keep information

  • Emails: only as long as we need them for the centennial and its records, unless the law requires us to keep them longer. Records of permissions and credits are kept as long as the material is published or archived.
  • Google Analytics: visitor-level data for two months; Google keeps combined reports longer.
  • Your analytics choice in your browser: 12 months, or until you clear it.

8. Security

The website is served over an encrypted (HTTPS) connection, and we limit access to our inbox to the volunteers who need it. Ordinary email is not fully secure, and no website or inbox can be guaranteed against every threat, so please do not send us sensitive information.

9. Children

This website is for a general audience and is not directed to children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If a student under 13 would like to take part, for example in student art, a parent, guardian or teacher should contact us. If you believe a child under 13 has sent us personal information, contact us and we will delete it.

10. California privacy notices

This policy is intended to meet the California Online Privacy Protection Act. We do not disclose personal information to third parties for their own direct-marketing purposes (California Civil Code section 1798.83). If you have a question about your privacy rights, contact us using the details below.

11. Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we will post the new version here with a new effective date. If a change affects how we use information we already have, we will also post a notice on the home page before it takes effect.
