Accessibility Statement

Making the centennial welcoming.

Everyone should be able to use this website and take part in the centennial. Here is what we do, what we know still needs work and how to reach us.

Last reviewed: October 17, 2026
Last updated: October 17, 2026

Our commitment

The Sherman Oaks 100 Centennial Committee (“the Committee,” “we”) wants people with disabilities to have the same access to information about the centennial as everyone else, consistent with the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA). This statement covers the website at shermanoaks100.com and the centennial events the Committee organizes.

The standard we follow

We design and test this website to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1, Level AA (opens in a new tab). This is the technical standard that the U.S. Department of Justice adopted for the websites of state and local governments under Title II of the ADA (28 C.F.R. part 35, subpart H). Where we can, we also follow the newer WCAG 2.2 guidance on focus visibility and target size.

Current status

In our October 16, 2026 review, the pages people use (the home page with its events, history, map, stories, support, get-involved, FAQ and contact sections, plus this statement and the privacy, terms, copyright and “page not found” pages) met WCAG 2.1 Level AA in the tests described below, apart from the limitations listed on this page. On October 17, 2026 we added three sponsorship pages (Centennial Series packages, single-event sponsorships and centennial opportunities) and the downloadable sponsorship flyer. We repeated the automated checks and screen-width checks on every page, and checked the new pages’ contrast, text spacing and keyboard focus. The flyer itself is listed under known limitations. We have not yet tested the site with people who use assistive technology, so we treat the site as partially conformant until that testing is done and any problems it finds are fixed.

What we built in

  • A “Skip to content” link, clear page landmarks and a logical heading structure.
  • Every control can be reached and used with a keyboard, and the focused item always has a visible outline that the sticky header and cookie notice do not cover.
  • Text colors meet the AA contrast ratios: at least 4.5:1 for regular text and 3:1 for large text and for focus outlines.
  • Pages can be zoomed to 200% and read on a 320-pixel-wide screen without scrolling sideways, and nothing is cut off when readers increase letter, word or line spacing.
  • Photographs have text descriptions. Decorative graphics are hidden from screen readers.
  • The then-and-now photo comparisons work with a keyboard (arrow keys) and screen readers, and both photographs are described.
  • Every place on the community map is also listed in text beside the map, with its address, description and visiting notes.
  • Animations are reduced when your device asks for reduced motion.
  • Links to other websites and to the sponsorship flyer open in a new tab and say so to screen readers. Email links open a new message in your email app. Information never depends on color alone.

How we tested

  • Automated checks with the axe-core accessibility engine (version 4.10) for WCAG 2.0, 2.1 and 2.2 Level A and AA rules.
  • A contrast check of every visible piece of text against its actual background, including text over gradients and photographs.
  • Manual checks of keyboard use, focus visibility, headings, link and button names, image descriptions, 320-pixel reflow, the WCAG text-spacing test and reduced motion.
  • Screen widths of 320, 375, 768 and 1,280 pixels.

Known limitations

  • Map imagery. The street map itself is a picture drawn from OpenStreetMap data, so screen readers cannot read the street names inside it. Every place on the map is listed in text beside it, with an address and a Directions link.
  • Historical photographs. Some archive photographs are faded or low in contrast because of their age. Each one has a text description, and we can describe any photograph in more detail on request.
  • Other websites. Links to OpenStreetMap, Google Maps and your email app take you to services we do not control. If one of them is hard to use, contact us and we will give you the information another way.
  • Event details. Venues, times and their accessibility information are not confirmed yet. We will add access details (such as step-free entry, seating, restrooms and parking) to each event as soon as we have them.
  • Assistive-technology testing. We have not yet tested the site with screen-reader, voice-control or switch users. Your feedback helps.
  • Sponsorship flyer (PDF). The two-page flyer linked from the sponsorship pages is a print design. It identifies English as its language, but it is not tagged for screen readers, so assistive technology may read it out of order. Every package, level, price, date and contact in it is also on the three sponsorship pages, and we can send it in another format on request.

Apart from that flyer, we do not publish videos, audio or downloadable documents. Any we add will have accurate captions or transcripts, or will be accessible documents, before they go online. If you need any information from this website in another format, such as large print or plain text, ask us and we will provide it.

Accessibility at centennial events

For events the Committee organizes, we will:

  • choose venues with accessible entrances, routes, seating and restrooms whenever we can, and post the access details with each event;
  • welcome service animals;
  • provide auxiliary aids and services, such as American Sign Language interpreters, real-time captioning (CART), assistive listening, large-print or electronic materials, and accessible seating, when you ask; and
  • never charge you for an accommodation.

Please ask at least two weeks before an event if you can, so we have time to arrange interpreters or captioning. We will still do our best with later requests. Some events are produced by partner organizations; we will pass your request to the right organizer and follow up with you.

Ask for help or report a barrier

Email info@shermanoaks100.com with the subject “Accessibility request.” It helps if you tell us:

  • the page or event, and what you were trying to do;
  • what went wrong, or the accommodation or format you need; and
  • the best way to reach you. If you would rather talk on the phone, include your number and we will call you.

If email is hard for you to use, anyone you choose, such as a friend, relative or support worker, may contact us for you.

We aim to reply within five business days. If we cannot fix a website problem right away, we will give you the information or service another way in the meantime. If you are not satisfied with our response, reply to the same message and ask for a review by the steering committee’s co-chairs.

Reviewing this statement

We will review this statement whenever the website changes significantly and at least once a year, and update the dates at the top of the page.
